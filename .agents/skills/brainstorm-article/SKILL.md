# Brainstorm an article

Evaluate the article's idea, outline, or draft without rewriting it unless the user asks for a rewrite.
Keep discussion focused on the reader, the article's central point, and how the story unfolds.

## Evaluate the narrative

Identify the intended reader and central point from the supplied material.
Check whether the introduction establishes why the topic matters to the author and reader.
Check whether each phase moves from a concrete problem through a decision to a change in practice.
For an evolving workflow, check that each phase adds a capability and that later sections depend on earlier changes.
Check whether the conclusion follows from the story and leaves the right scope of claim.

## Give actionable feedback

Surface ambiguities, coherence gaps, unsupported claims, missing transitions, and repeated points.
Explain why each issue matters to a reader.
Offer concrete alternative phrasing or section structures for places that need sharpening.
Keep examples general across tasks when requested, while showing the friction the author encountered while using the workflow.
Distinguish facts the author needs to supply from editorial choices the author can make.
Ask only for information needed to avoid inventing the author's experience.

Treat workflow notation as visual source material when the author says it should not appear in article content.
Respect instructions to preserve selected edits or leave other passages unchanged.
End with a short list of open choices only when the author needs to decide before drafting.
